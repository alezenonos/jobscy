Cyprus Job Market Visualiser GitHub

This is a research tool that visualises 39 ISCO-08 occupation groups in the Cyprus labour market, using Eurostat employment (Labour Force Survey) and earnings (Structure of Earnings Survey) data, plus the official job-openings forecasts of the HRDA (2022–2032) and Cedefop (2022–2035). Each rectangle's area is proportional to total employment. Colour shows the selected metric — toggle between growth outlook, average pay (EUR), job openings, education requirements, AI exposure, how distinctly Cypriot each job is, and pay compared with the EU and Greece. Growth outlook and AI exposure are LLM estimates; the job-openings layer uses the official forecasts. This is not a report, a paper, or a serious economic publication — it is a development tool for exploring Cyprus employment data visually.

About the data, methods and caveats

LLM-powered colouring: The source code includes data pipelines, parsers, and a framework for writing custom LLM prompts to score and colour occupations by any criteria. You write a prompt, the LLM scores each occupation, and the treemap colours accordingly. The "Digital AI Exposure" option is one example — it estimates how much current AI (which is primarily digital) will reshape each occupation. But you could write a different prompt for any question — e.g. exposure to humanoid robotics, offshoring risk, climate impact — and re-run the pipeline to get a different colouring.

View the Digital AI Exposure scoring prompt (example)
You are an expert labour market analyst evaluating how exposed different occupations are to AI. You will be given an occupation description based on the ISCO-08 international classification, in the context of the Cyprus and EU labour market. Rate the occupation's overall AI Exposure on a scale from 0 to 10. AI Exposure measures: how much will AI reshape this occupation? Consider both direct effects (AI automating tasks currently done by humans) and indirect effects (AI making each worker so productive that fewer are needed). A key signal is whether the job's work product is fundamentally digital. If the job can be done entirely from a home office on a computer — writing, coding, analysing, communicating — then AI exposure is inherently high (7+), because AI capabilities in digital domains are advancing rapidly. Conversely, jobs requiring physical presence, manual skill, or real-time human interaction in the physical world have a natural barrier to AI exposure. Consider the Cyprus/EU context: tourism and hospitality are major employers, financial services and shipping are key sectors, the EU Digital Decade targets drive digital skills demand, and the public sector is proportionally larger than in other economies. Use these anchors to calibrate your score: - 0–1: Minimal exposure. Examples: agricultural labourer, construction labourer, commercial diver. - 2–3: Low exposure. Examples: electrician, plumber, firefighter, building trades worker. - 4–5: Moderate exposure. Examples: health associate professional, police officer, personal care worker. - 6–7: High exposure. Examples: teaching professional, manager, business administration professional. - 8–9: Very high exposure. Examples: ICT professional, legal professional, business/admin associate professional. - 10: Maximum exposure. Examples: general clerk, numerical recording clerk, data entry operator. Respond with ONLY a JSON object in this exact format, no other text: {"exposure": <0-10>, "rationale": "<2-3 sentences explaining the key factors>"}

Caveat on pay: Eurostat's Structure of Earnings Survey (2022, enterprises with 10+ employees) only publishes Cyprus pay by ISCO-08 major group, so every occupation in a group shows the same figure. "Average pay" is mean gross annual earnings including the 13th salary and bonuses, carried forward to 2025 as an estimate using the growth in Cyprus's wage index (Eurostat labour cost index, wages and salaries, +15.5% from 2022 to 2025). The tooltip also shows the survey's own 2022 figure. The range shows the 10th to 90th percentile of hourly pay in that major group, annualised with the same ratio, so it is an approximation. Pay by sector comes from the same survey and leaves out sectors with fewer than 500 employees in the group. The next survey wave (2026) is expected to be published around 2028.

About job openings: Openings count both new jobs (expansion demand) and jobs freed up by people retiring or leaving (replacement demand). The colour shows HRDA's projected openings a year as a share of the group's 2022 employment. HRDA's Forecasts of Employment Needs in Cyprus 2022–2032 and Cedefop's Skills Forecast use different models and periods, so their numbers differ; both are shown in the tooltip.

About the EU comparisons: "Distinctly Cypriot" divides each group's share of all jobs in Cyprus by its share of all jobs in the EU27 (Eurostat Labour Force Survey, same year), a measure economists call a location quotient: 2× means the job is twice as common in Cyprus as in the EU, 0.5× half as common. Groups marked as estimates in the tooltip have small, partly suppressed Cyprus figures, so read them with care. "Pay vs EU" compares mean annual earnings for the whole major group in the same 2022 survey (enterprises with 10+ employees), in euros and in purchasing power standards (PPS), which adjust for the lower price level in Cyprus. It uses the 2022 survey figures for all three, not the 2025 estimate.

Caveat on Growth Outlook: The 10-year outlook is an LLM estimate. Where the tooltip shows historical growth, the model was given that Eurostat figure for Cyprus; otherwise it worked from the occupation group alone. It is not an official projection and should be read as a rough indication only. The tooltip shows HRDA's and Cedefop's official employment forecasts alongside it for comparison.

Caveat on Digital AI Exposure scores: These are rough LLM estimates, not rigorous predictions. A high score does not predict the job will disappear. Software developers score 9/10 because AI is transforming their work — but demand for software could easily grow as each developer becomes more productive. The score does not account for demand elasticity, latent demand, regulatory barriers, or social preferences for human workers. Many high-exposure jobs will be reshaped, not replaced.
